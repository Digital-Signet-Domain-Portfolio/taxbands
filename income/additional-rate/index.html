Additional-rate tax band (45%)

Reviewed by Oliver Wakefield-Smith, Founder, Digital Signet. Rates read from HMRC, the VOA, the Scottish Government and the OBR.Topic: tax bands
rates verified 24 Jul 2026 · next refresh after Autumn Statement
Direct answer

The 45% additional rate of income tax applies to taxable income above £125,140 in 2026/271. The threshold was reduced from £150,000 to £125,140 on 6 April 2023, deliberately lined up with the income level at which the personal allowance is fully withdrawn5.

Why £125,140?

The personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 over £100,000. £12,570 of allowance is fully gone by £125,140 (100,000 + 12,570 × 2). Setting the additional-rate threshold at the same point closes the discontinuity between the 60% trap and the 45% band.

How dividends and savings work at this band

Dividends above £125,140 are taxed at the additional dividend rate of 39.35%, unchanged in 2026/276. Savings interest at this band sits in the additional-rate income band and has no Personal Savings Allowance7.

Compared to Scotland

Scotland charges 48% above £125,140 – its Top rate – three percentage points more than rUK14.
